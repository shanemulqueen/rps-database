Programs

Renewable Portfolio Standard — Tier 1

RPS MD Public Service Commission (PSC) Md. Code, Public Utilities § 7-701 et seq. Applies to: EDC, Retail Supplier
Tier 1 covers new renewable resources. Target is 50% by 2030 per the Clean Energy Jobs Act. Solar has a dedicated carve-out (14.5% by 2028) within the Tier 1 obligation. Offshore wind has a separate OREC carve-out (2.5%).

Legislation

Renewable Energy Portfolio Standard (enacted 2004)  enacted 2004
Original RPS enactment establishing Tier 1 and Tier 2 framework
2013 Maryland Offshore Wind Energy Act (Chapter 3, Acts of 2013)
Added OREC carve-out of 2.5% within Tier 1; established offshore wind procurement framework
2019 Clean Energy Jobs Act (Chapter 757, Acts of 2019)
Increased RPS from 25% to 50% by 2030; raised solar carve-out from 2% to 14.5% by 2028; removed offshore wind development cap
2021 RPS Amendment (Chapter 673, Acts of 2021)
Adjusted solar carve-out schedule for 2022–2029; raised Solar ACP; removed black liquor from Tier 1 eligibility
2022 Climate Solutions Now Act (Chapter 38, Acts of 2022)
Set 60% GHG reduction by 2031 and net-zero by 2045 as statutory goals; reinforced RPS trajectory

Renewable Portfolio Standard — Tier 2

RPS MD Public Service Commission (PSC) Md. Code, Public Utilities § 7-701 et seq. Applies to: EDC, Retail Supplier
Tier 2 covers existing, older clean energy resources — primarily conventional hydro and poultry litter facilities. The Tier 2 requirement is 2.5% and has remained relatively static.

Legislation

Established Tier 2 for pre-2004 clean energy resources

Solar Carve-Out (SREC Program)

RPS MD Public Service Commission (PSC) Md. Code, Public Utilities § 7-703 Applies to: EDC, Retail Supplier
Maryland's solar carve-out requires a separate percentage of retail sales from solar PV. SRECs are traded in the PJM-GATS market. The Brighter Tomorrow Act (2024) enhanced the SREC multiplier and expanded access. Current target escalates to 14.5% by 2028. Retail suppliers must purchase SRECs or pay the Solar Alternative Compliance Payment (SACP).

Legislation

Clean Energy Jobs Act (Chapter 757, Acts of 2019)  enacted 2019
Raised solar carve-out from 2% to 5.5% rising to 14.5% by 2028; significantly expanded the SREC market
2021 RPS Amendment (Chapter 673, Acts of 2021)
Decreased solar carve-out requirements for 2022–2029; increased SACP ceiling to $60 (2023–2024) and $55 (2025)
2024 Brighter Tomorrow Act (Chapter 595, Acts of 2024)
Enhanced SREC multiplier (3 SRECs per 2 MWh = 150%); up to $7,500 per eligible household; expedited permitting via SolarApp+; raised net metering cap

Offshore Wind — OREC Program

PROCUREMENT MD Public Service Commission (PSC) / MD Energy Administration (MEA) Md. Code, Public Utilities § 7-704.1 Applies to: BGE, Pepco, Delmarva Power, Potomac Edison
Maryland's offshore wind program uses Offshore Renewable Energy Certificates (ORECs) — a state-administered procurement. PSC selects projects and approves OREC prices. EDCs are required to purchase ORECs from approved projects. ORECs qualify for the Tier 1 RPS OREC carve-out (2.5% of retail sales). Current goal is 8.5 GW by 2031 per the POWER Act (2023).

Legislation

Maryland Offshore Wind Energy Act (Chapter 3, Acts of 2013)  enacted 2013
Created OREC mechanism; set initial 2.5% carve-out (~500 MW); established application and approval process for offshore wind projects
2019 Clean Energy Jobs Act (Chapter 757, Acts of 2019)
Removed 2.5% upper limit on offshore wind development; required at least 1,200 MW of Round 2 projects; established three procurement rounds
2023 Promoting Offshore Wind Energy Resources (POWER) Act (Chapter 95, Acts of 2023)
Set 8.5 GW goal by 2031; required community benefit agreements; directed state facilities to procure offshore wind via PPAs
2024 Offshore Wind Projects Act Amendment (Chapter 431, Acts of 2024)
Revised Round 2 procurement schedule; required in-state expenditure commitments; enabled OREC reallocation between projects

Procurement Requirements

PSC issues competitive solicitations for offshore wind projects. Developers submit OREC price applications. PSC evaluates based on economic impact, ratepayer cost, environmental benefit, and in-state job creation. Approved projects receive 20-year OREC contracts. Community benefit agreements required for Round 2 projects. EDCs must purchase ORECs from all PSC-approved projects.
