Programs

Class I RPS

RPS CT PURA C.G.S. § 16-245a Applies to: EDC, Retail Supplier
Class I covers new renewable resources that began commercial operation on or after July 1, 2003. Under PA 18-50 the standard had been scheduled to climb toward roughly 40% by 2030, but Public Act 23-102 (2023) cut the Class I requirement by 7 percentage points for compliance years 2026 through 2030 to ease ratepayer cost — the requirement drops from 30% (2025) to 25% (2026) and rises to about 29% by 2030. Municipal electric utilities are subject to a separate, lower standard under § 16-245a(b).

Legislation

An Act Concerning Electric Restructuring (Public Act 98-28)  enacted 1998
Original RPS enactment; established Class I and Class II framework
2013 An Act Concerning Connecticut's Clean Energy Goals (Public Act 13-303)
Expanded Class I eligibility; prohibited interstate REC double-counting; authorized DEEP to issue competitive RFPs
2018 An Act Concerning Connecticut's Energy Future (Public Act 18-50)
Increased Class I target to 26% by 2023, escalating toward 40% by 2030; replaced net metering with tariff-based programs
2023 An Act Concerning the Disposal of Renewable Energy Credits (Public Act 23-102)
Reduced the Class I requirement by 7 percentage points for 2026–2030 (e.g., 32%→25% in 2026, ending near 29% in 2030); directed PURA to establish procedures to dispose of RECs purchased under renewable tariffs and procurements; directed the state to procure new zero-carbon Class I resources equal to 7% of load on top of the RPS.

Class II RPS

RPS CT PURA C.G.S. § 16-245a Applies to: EDC, Retail Supplier
Class II covers older and waste-derived resources — primarily permitted trash-to-energy (MSW) facilities, certain existing biomass, and hydro that predates the July 1, 2003 Class I commercial-operation cutoff. Statute frames this as a "Class I or Class II" adder of 3% (suppliers may also meet it with surplus Class I). NOTE: LBNL data encodes the Class II line at 4% — discrepancy flagged for review.

Legislation

An Act Concerning Electric Restructuring (Public Act 98-28)  enacted 1998
Established Class II tier for pre-2004 eligible resources

Class III RPS

RPS CT PURA C.G.S. § 16-245a(a)(2) Applies to: EDC, Retail Supplier
Class III is a distinct efficiency / combined-heat-and-power standard, separate from the renewable Class I and Class II tiers. The requirement began at 1% in 2008, rose to 3% in 2009 and 4% on and after January 1, 2010, and has remained flat at 4% since. It is met with Class III certificates (C3RECs) for: customer-sided CHP (min. 50% operating efficiency) installed on/after Jan 1, 2006; verified electricity savings from conservation & load-management programs begun on/after Jan 1, 2006 (ratepayer-funded program savings are excluded from 2014); and waste-heat / waste-pressure recovery systems installed on/after April 1, 2007.

Legislation

An Act Concerning Electric Restructuring (Public Act 98-28)  enacted 1998
RPS framework; Class III for CHP, conservation and load management added by subsequent energy legislation (notably PA 07-242)
2007 An Act Concerning Electricity and Energy Efficiency (Public Act 07-242)
Established the Class III standard (1% in 2008 rising to 4% by 2010) for customer CHP, conservation & load management, and waste-heat recovery.

Generation Requirements

Class III certificates (C3RECs) are issued by NEPOOL GIS. CHP systems must meet the 50% minimum operating-efficiency standard; conservation and load-management savings are verified under PURA-approved measurement and verification protocols.

Zero-Carbon Procurement (Millstone)

PROCUREMENT CT DEEP / CT PURA C.G.S. § 16a-3m Applies to: Eversource CT, United Illuminating
Not a certificate/credit program. DEEP is authorized to solicit long-term power purchase agreements from zero-carbon generators. In 2019, Millstone Nuclear Power Station (Dominion) secured a 10-year PPA for ~9 million MWh/year (approx. 50% of output) at $49.99/MWh, running through 2029, with distribution companies required to enter the PURA-approved PPAs. Separately, Public Act 23-102 (2023) directs the state to procure new zero-carbon Class I resources equal to 7% of load, in addition to the RPS.

Legislation

An Act Concerning Zero Carbon Procurement (Public Act 17-3, Special Session)  enacted 2017
Authorized DEEP to solicit zero-carbon resource contracts; directed study of Millstone economic viability; established 12 million MWh annual cap

Procurement Requirements

DEEP issues competitive solicitations for zero-carbon electricity. Contracts are long-term PPAs (3–10 years for nuclear/hydro). PURA reviews and approves all selected contracts. Distribution companies take title to the energy under the approved PPAs. Total annual contracted volume capped at 12 million MWh.
