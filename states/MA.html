Programs

Class I RPS

RPS MA DOER M.G.L. c. 25A § 11F Applies to: IOU, CCA, Retail Supplier
Municipal utilities are exempt from RPS and CES obligations. Class I solar carve-out is served by SREC I (retired), SREC II (transitioning), and SMART (current). Solar targets are denominated in MW and translated to equivalent % of retail sales.

Legislation

An Act Relative to Restructuring the Electric Utility Industry (Chapter 164, Acts of 1997)  enacted 1997
Original RPS enactment
An Act Relative to Green Communities (Chapter 169, Acts of 2008)  enacted 2008
Established separate Class I and Class II tiers; replaced unified RPS framework
2018 An Act to Advance Clean Energy (Chapter 227, Acts of 2018)
Increased Class I annual growth rate from 1% to 2%; targeted 35% by 2030
2021 An Act Creating a Next-Generation Roadmap for Massachusetts Climate Policy (Chapter 8, Acts of 2021)
Further increased RPS to 40% by 2030; Section 32 amendment to c. 25A § 11F

Generation Requirements

Hourly matching required starting 2025 for new contracts. RECs must be generated in the same clock hour as the load they serve. Pre-2025 contracts may continue under legacy annual matching through contract expiration.

Class II RPS

RPS MA DOER M.G.L. c. 25A § 11F Applies to: IOU, CCA, Retail Supplier
Class II covers existing (pre-1998) renewable generation and MSW facilities. Targets are set annually by DOER based on historical output.

Legislation

An Act Relative to Green Communities (Chapter 169, Acts of 2008)  enacted 2008
Established Class II tier for pre-1998 renewables and MSW

Clean Energy Standard — New (CES)

CES MA DOER / MassDEP M.G.L. c. 21N (Global Warming Solutions Act); 310 CMR 7.75 Applies to: IOU, CCA, Retail Supplier
Requires retail suppliers to procure a percentage of sales from new (post-2018) clean energy resources, including nuclear and large hydro not eligible under RPS. CES obligation is incremental to the RPS. Administered under the Global Warming Solutions Act, not the RPS statute.

Legislation

An Act Establishing the Global Warming Solutions Act (Chapter 298, Acts of 2008)  enacted 2008
Statutory authority for CES; established GHG reduction mandates
310 CMR 7.75 — Clean Energy Standard (regulatory promulgation)  enacted 2017
Regulation adopted August 2017 establishing CES requirements; 16% in 2018 rising 2% annually to 80% in 2050
2021 An Act Creating a Next-Generation Roadmap for Massachusetts Climate Policy (Chapter 8, Acts of 2021)
Section 34 codified CES reference; increased offshore wind procurement to 5,600 MW

Generation Requirements

Hourly matching applies to CES certificates on the same schedule as Class I RPS. Imported clean energy (e.g., from Quebec hydro) eligible if delivered over a dedicated transmission line or accompanied by a long-term contract with delivery verification.

Procurement Requirements

Clean Energy Certificates (CECs) must be sourced from generators that began commercial operation after January 1, 2018. Long-term contracts (10–20 years) encouraged but not required.

Clean Energy Standard — Existing (CES-E)

CES MA DOER / MassDEP M.G.L. c. 21N; 310 CMR 7.75 Applies to: IOU, CCA, Retail Supplier
CES-E covers existing (pre-2018) clean energy resources, primarily existing nuclear and large hydro. Targets are set to phase down as new CES resources ramp up.

Alternative Portfolio Standard (APS)

APS MA DOER M.G.L. c. 25A § 11F½ Applies to: IOU, CCA, Retail Supplier
The APS is a parallel standard to the RPS requiring retail suppliers to source a percentage of sales from alternative energy resources — primarily combined heat and power (CHP), flywheel storage, and demand response. Not tracked in LBNL RPS data. Targets are set separately from the RPS.

Legislation

An Act Relative to Green Communities (Chapter 169, Acts of 2008)  enacted 2008
Created APS as a parallel standard to the RPS; effective January 1, 2009
2014 Chapter 251, Acts of 2014
Added renewable thermal technologies to APS eligibility
2016 An Act to Promote Energy Diversity (Chapter 188, Acts of 2016)
Further expanded APS eligibility; added energy storage authorization

Generation Requirements

Alternative Energy Credits (AECs) are issued by NEPOOL GIS. CHP systems must meet minimum efficiency thresholds (42.5% for natural gas). Demand response resources must be registered with ISO-NE.
