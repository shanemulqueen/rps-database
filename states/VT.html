Programs

Renewable Energy Standard (RES) — Tiers I, II, IV & V

RPS VT PUC / VT DPS 30 V.S.A. § 8002–8005 Applies to: Green Mountain Power, Burlington Electric, other Vermont utilities
Vermont's RES is structured in five tiers (post-Act 179, 2024), not program classes. Tier I is the general renewable energy requirement; Tier II is distributed generation (≤5 MW, post-June 2015); Tier IV (new in 2024) is new regional renewables such as offshore wind; Tier V applies to already-100%-renewable utilities for load growth. Tier III (energy transformation) is a separate program below. The 100% renewable electricity target was accelerated to 2030–2035 by Act 179 (2024), passed over the governor's veto.

Legislation

An Act Relating to Establishing a Renewable Energy Standard (Act 56 of 2015)  enacted 2015
Original RES enactment; created three-tier framework (Tier I general, Tier II distributed, Tier III transformation); Tier I at 55% rising to 75% by 2032; effective January 1, 2017
2024 An Act Relating to the Renewable Energy Standard (Act 179 of 2024 / H.289)
Accelerated Tier I to 63% by 2025 rising to 100% by 2030–2035; accelerated Tier II to 5.8% by 2025; added Tier IV (new regional renewables) and Tier V (load growth for 100%-renewable utilities); new biomass/hydro restrictions; passed over governor's veto

Generation Requirements

Tier II resources must be ≤5 MW nameplate and commissioned after June 30, 2015, located in Vermont or a utility's service territory, and directly interconnected. Tier IV resources must be new (post-2010) regional renewables outside Vermont (e.g., offshore wind, out-of-state solar) delivered to Vermont load.

Farm methane and landfill gas eligible for Tier I. Also eligible for Tier III as energy transformation if used for heating/transportation.

Waste-to-energy with qualifying renewable fraction may be eligible. Subject to emissions and efficiency standards.
Geothermal Eligible
Electric geothermal eligible for Tier I. Ground-source heat pumps may qualify under Tier III (energy transformation).
Storage Conditional
Paired with a Tier I renewable generator. Standalone storage qualifies under Tier III (energy transformation) for load-shifting of renewable energy.
Nuclear / Zero-Carbon Not Eligible
Vermont Yankee retired 2014. No active nuclear in Vermont. No nuclear eligibility under VT RES.
Fuel Cell Conditional
Fuel cells using biogas or renewable hydrogen eligible for Tier I. Natural gas fuel cells may qualify under Tier III only.

Renewable Energy Standard — Tier III (Energy Transformation)

RPS VT DPS / VT PUC 30 V.S.A. § 8004 Applies to: Green Mountain Power, Burlington Electric, other Vermont utilities
Tier III is a unique obligation requiring utilities to demonstrate that their customers are reducing fossil fuel use through electrification and efficiency measures — not just procuring renewable generation. It covers EVs, heat pumps, weatherization, fuel-switching, and other demand-side measures. 7.33% of retail sales equivalent by 2025. This is Vermont's primary mechanism for decarbonizing heat and transportation alongside the electric sector.

Legislation

An Act Relating to Establishing a Renewable Energy Standard (Act 56 of 2015)  enacted 2015
Created Tier III as the energy transformation obligation; 2% starting 2017, rising to 12% by 2032
2024 An Act Relating to the Renewable Energy Standard (Act 179 of 2024 / H.289)
Accelerated Tier III to 7.33% by 2025; maintained 12% target by 2032

Generation Requirements

Tier III credits are not RECs — they represent verified fossil fuel savings from customer-side measures. Utilities earn Tier III credit by deploying or financing: electric vehicles and charging infrastructure, heat pumps (air-source and ground-source), weatherization and building efficiency, fuel-switching from oil/propane to electricity, biofuels for thermal use, and demand response with renewable integration. Savings are measured and verified per DPS protocols.

Standard Offer Program

PROCUREMENT VT PUC / VT DPS 30 V.S.A. § 8005a Applies to: Green Mountain Power, Burlington Electric
Vermont's Standard Offer is a feed-in tariff for small renewable generators (≤2.2 MW). Generators receive long-term fixed-price contracts instead of selling into the wholesale market or REC markets. Projects commissioned after September 30, 2009, are eligible. Annual RFP capacity tranches set by PUC (10 MW/year in recent rounds). Administered by VEPP Inc. as program manager.

Legislation

Act 45 of 2009 (original SPEED program framework)  enacted 2009
Created Standard Offer/SPEED framework for small renewable feed-in tariffs
2012 Vermont Energy Act of 2012 (Act 170)
Expanded capacity from 50 MW to 127.5 MW total; introduced market-based pricing mechanism

Procurement Requirements

Annual competitive solicitations (RFP released April 1st each year). Projects ≤2.2 MW, commissioned after September 30, 2009. Winning projects receive 25-year fixed-price contracts with Green Mountain Power or Burlington Electric. Pricing set through PUC competitive procurement process.
